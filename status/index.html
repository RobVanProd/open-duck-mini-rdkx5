What the evidence says

Scalar tuning did not solve behavior emergence

  1. Learning rate, entropy, imitation scale, discount, and unroll searches produced no persistent gait and no full checkpoint pass.
  2. Four structurally different policy/critic mechanisms also failed the frozen advancement rule.
  3. The shared-setup audit found the missing clean bootstrap: full randomization, delays, noise, and pushes were active from update one. Reset also used a zero-norm phase feature and a single-support phase inconsistent with home.
  4. A corrected nominal phase-0 / phase-20 bootstrap still produced only one isolated moving pass per arm and six hard failures each. Curriculum cleanup and phase alignment are insufficient.
  5. Signed progress preserved x=.074 seed 100 at both 3M and 4M, but still had six hard failures, two saturated x=.08 runs per checkpoint pair, and no full checkpoint pass. It is not a winner.
  6. Recorded return reconstructs to within 2.83e-5 at mature checkpoints, so clipping is not selected. Alive plus yaw tracking are 79.81–81.55% of final positive-scaled reward mass and are now the evidence-selected factor.
  7. Progress-conditioned survival tied O1 exactly: 2/8 moving passes, one persistent seed, six hard failures, and zero full checkpoint passes. The reward-objective family is closed.
  8. Direct reference execution is not a valid cloning label: every audited form moved backward on average, with 2–4 falls per 8 runs. The selected formulation anchors the actor at the projected reference and learns state-feedback residuals.
  9. The reference-residual software contract passed on CPU: initial JAX and ONNX actions match the projected reference within 2.98e-8, PPO log probability is finite, and a 1,024-step training smoke exported the correct final-action graph.
